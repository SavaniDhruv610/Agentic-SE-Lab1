The group forecast

AI gets the keyboard.
Humans keep the keys.

Our shared view: AI is a power tool, not an autopilot for judgment.

Across the team, AI already helps with boilerplate, prototypes, scripts, tests, explanations, and exploring possible solutions. That means less time typing every line and more time deciding what deserves to exist—and checking whether what got built actually works.

In the next 5–10 years, we expect more agents and automation in development, delivery, and quality assurance. Engineers will spend more energy on requirements, architecture, product thinking, reliability, accessibility, and verification. Code may get cheaper; responsibility does not.

Where we agreeAI can accelerate the first draft. People still need to understand, test, and review the result.
What we watch closelyDhruv and Harshil stress reliability and security; Jevin worries about learning by doing; Pooja centers tests and data; Yashik calls out user context and accessibility.

Team policy, unofficial: Let the machine suggest. Let the humans ask “does this make sense?” Let the tests be the final duck-shaped jury.
